Title

Interactive Ouija Escape-Room Puzzle

Sheet 06 of 08
Dates
Jan 2025 – May 2025
Context
Cornerstone Engineering 2 (GE 1502), Northeastern University; small group of four
Role
Electronics wiring and box design within the group, plus Narrative Committee member
Tools
Hall effect sensors · solenoid lock · transistor and diode driver · arcade-style button · LEDs · Arduino (C++) · laser cutting · 3D printing · vinyl cutting

Wired the electronics for a Ouija-board escape-room puzzle in which a magnet in the planchette spells a six-letter word over Hall effect sensors and trips a solenoid lock. At the event it worked without a failure, and players solved it on their first attempt.

Objective

Build one escape-room puzzle that fits the shared storyline: a Ouija board that unlocks only after players spell a secret word. It was one puzzle in a class-wide escape room with a haunted-motel murder-mystery storyline.

How a player solves it

Players decode a riddle card, complete with red herrings, to find a six-letter word, then spell it by moving a 3D-printed planchette across the board. A magnet inside the planchette triggers Hall effect sensors in the board. When the word is right, a solenoid lock opens a trap-door box holding two keys.

What I did

  • Designed how the electronics connected across the large board box and the smaller trap-door box, and helped with the look of both.
  • Wired the solenoid lock: crimped the wires, built the breadboard circuit, and installed the transistor and diode.
  • Wired the arcade-style button and LED setup.
  • Helped plan the lettering and its placement on the board, painted the blood-style handprints, and helped with the fabric visuals.
  • On the Narrative Committee, helped write the overall plot and keep every puzzle consistent with it, came up with a word puzzle where players pull a 4-digit code out of the story text, and wrote the "bad ending" for players who fail to escape.

Built by the team

A laser-cut, painted wooden Ouija box; a smaller trap-door box holding two keys; vinyl-letter decals, painted symbols, and drapery; and a 3D-printed planchette that holds the magnet, finished in glossy paint to look like wet blood. A teammate led the coding after Milestone 2, another led the laser cutting and fabrication, and another the board's visual design.

Result

In testing the solenoid was finicky: it needed manual resets and sometimes failed. At the event, though, the puzzle worked without a failure. Players solved it on their first attempt, and one asked how it was built.

What I learned

How Hall effect sensors work; how to wire transistors, diodes, and solenoids; how to troubleshoot the vinyl cutter; and better C++ logic. A planned Wordle-style LED letter feedback had to be cut because of wiring and battery limits.

What I'd do differently

Plan the wiring and battery capacity early, so features like the Wordle-style feedback don't get cut.
